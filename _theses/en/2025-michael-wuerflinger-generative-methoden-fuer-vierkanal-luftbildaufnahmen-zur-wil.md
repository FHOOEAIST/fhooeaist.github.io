Wildlife Monitoring using drone technology provides valuable insights into animal populations and also their habitats. Since drone flights are time consuming and need proper preparation, this Bachelor Thesis aims to expand existing Data artificial using image generation. It generates high quality data, that is also able to generate a diverse range of new images. As pretrained models use 3 channels instead of the required 4 channels (3 for RGB, 1 for thermal), the model was trained from scratch. This meant that an existing image generation model had to be extended to 4 channels.

In the first step, unlabeled RGB images and also 4-channel images were generated. The fourth Channel included the associated thermal image from the RGB version. The results demonstrated that new content could be generated by using unlabeled data. However the generated pictures weren’t diverse, because it focuses on the generic features from the training dataset. Outpainting, that regenerates parts of the images, worked really well. In a further step, detailed image descriptions were used during the training. Though, the descriptions were to detailed and that high variability made the image quality worse. Therefore only short key words were used instead of the whole sentences.

This work shows that even with limited resources, like a small dataset and limited hardware and also a rather short training duration, realistic looking RGB pictures can be generated. Generating the thermal images resulted in unrealistic-looking images, while outpainting and reconstruction achieved acceptable looking results. It is difficult to determine whether a generated thermal image can be considered realistic, as there are no visual cues for this. Therefore only a comparison with real thermal images can be used as a reference. Future work could focus more on the targeted generation of wild animals. With this additional step the data basis for recognition and analysis can be further improved.
