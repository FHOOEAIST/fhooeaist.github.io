Companies increasingly expect AI systems to deliver reliable assistance directly from the unstructured documents accumulated on their shared drives. What is deployed in practice is typically a chunk-based retrieval pipeline in which a large language model reformulates loosely connected text fragments, an integration pattern whose hallucination risk is unacceptable on a technical shop floor. This paper proposes an alternative: introducing AI support incrementally by anchoring all knowledge artifacts (instructions, documents, employee feedback, questions and answers) to defined process steps. The approach is formulated as four design principles around a central idea: such a system is a participation structure, in which managers and operatives externalize knowledge as an embedded part of their routine work rather than as an overt documentation duty, while the bounded retrieval scope created by process anchoring counters known failure modes of retrieval-augmented generation. We present PAKA, a dual-interface prototype instantiating the approach, and report a formative two-month pilot in machine maintenance at an injection molding company. The pilot supported the manager-side participation mechanism, yielding an unplanned consolidation of the maintenance landscape into few standard processes, while operative-side participation remained sparse, surfacing accountability as a key adoption barrier. We position this work as a concept paper and outline the longitudinal, multi-company evaluation program required for full validation.
