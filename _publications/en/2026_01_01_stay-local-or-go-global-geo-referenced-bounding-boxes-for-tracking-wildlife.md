Drone-based thermal imaging is increasingly used for wildlife monitoring, but tracking individual animals in aerial footage remains difficult in forested habitats due to low texture, occlusions, and dynamic viewpoints. Traditional image-space tracking methods often fail because motion and appearance cues degrade in thermal data. This work investigates geo-referenced, rather than purely image-space, bounding boxes for tracking wildlife in thermal drone videos, comparing local (image-space) and global (geo-referenced) tracking strategies.
