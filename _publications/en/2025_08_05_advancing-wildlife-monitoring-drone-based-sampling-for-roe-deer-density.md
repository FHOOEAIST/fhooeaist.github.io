We use unmanned aerial drones to estimate wildlife density in southeastern Austria and compare these estimates to camera trap data. Using thermal (IR) and RGB imagery, drones enable efficient, non-intrusive animal counting. Surveys were conducted during the leafless period on single days in October and November 2024 in three areas of a sub-Illyrian hill and terrace landscape. Animals were manually identified in the recorded footage, and three density extrapolation approaches of increasing sophistication were applied: basic area-based methods, bootstrapping, and zero-inflated negative binomial modeling. Results were compared with Random Encounter Model estimates derived from camera trap data. Drone-based approaches generally produced higher density estimates than the comparison method, with one exception in October. Both methodologies provide valuable but distinct perspectives on animal populations; drones offer a promising, scalable method for wildlife density estimation.
