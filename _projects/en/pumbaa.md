## Pumbaa

African Swine Fever (ASF) is steadily advancing toward Austria, and Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza reached a record level across Europe in 2025/26 – for the first time also affecting a poultry farm in Upper Austria. Outbreaks of both diseases require the establishment of quarantine zones and a systematic search for dead animals, as the virus remains infectious in carcasses for months. Existing methods such as human search lines, cadaver detection dogs, or drones with thermal cameras, however, are cost-intensive, time-consuming, and ineffective for older carcasses: thermal cameras only work for about two weeks after an animal's death. At the same time, there are no integrated solutions for monitoring quarantine zones, whose effectiveness depends on consistently keeping unauthorized persons out. The project PUMBAA (Platform for Unmanned Measurement Systems for the Assessment of Airborne Agents) therefore develops an integrated drone-based platform for animal disease management that, for the first time, combines automated carcass detection with person detection for quarantine surveillance. Its core innovations are gas sensor-based carcass detection via decomposition gases such as cadaverine, putrescine, and skatole, which can locate older carcasses independent of temperature differences; deployable sensor nodes ("Digi-Noses") for long-term monitoring of suspected areas; AI-driven adaptive flight planning based on measurement data, terrain, and wind; deep reinforcement learning-based coordination of drone swarms for efficient area coverage; multimodal fusion of gas, thermal, and visual data; and AI-based person and wildlife detection. The technology is deliberately disease-agnostic and can be deployed for ASF in forests as well as for avian influenza along waterways; beyond that, it offers potential for disaster response and search-and-rescue operations. The AIST research group is supporting colleagues of the partner research group, the [Digital Media Lab](https://digitalmedialab.at/), in this effort.
