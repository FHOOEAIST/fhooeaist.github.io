AIST was represented with two talks at ISM 2026, the 8th International Conference on Industry of the Future and Smart Manufacturing, held in Padua and Venice from 11 to 13 November. Martin Hanreich and Mert Dalkilic presented recent work on applying large language models (LLMs) in manufacturing and process management.

Martin presented the paper PAKA: A Process-Anchored Knowledge Assistant for Manufacturing Processes. Many companies expect AI assistants to be a kind of "miracle machine" that, once pointed at the documents a company already has, answers any question from any department. PAKA instead anchors the assistant's knowledge in the concrete manufacturing processes, making it a more reliable aid in day-to-day production.

Mert presented the paper Template-Guided BPMN Process Generation with Traceable LLM Output and Deterministic Guardrails. The pipeline generates BPMN process models from natural-language requirements by reusing tasks from existing process templates, so that every step can be traced back to its source. A three-phase validation and rule-based, deterministic repair replace the error-prone self-repair of the language model. In an evaluation with 36 runs, on average 80% of the generated tasks were taken directly from templates, and all processes were free of structural errors after an average of 1.3 repair iterations.

Together, the two papers show how grounding generative AI in existing process knowledge and combining it with deterministic checks makes it traceable and reliable in industrial applications.
