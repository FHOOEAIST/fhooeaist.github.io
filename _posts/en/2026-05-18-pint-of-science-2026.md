At the Pint of Science Festival 2026, Christoph gave the talk "On the trail of BAMBI and THUMPER" on 18 May at the Forty Nine bar in Linz, in the track "The Wild and the Wired": science over a pint, in a bar instead of a lecture hall.

Under the deliberately provocative claim "We need more surveillance!", the talk asked how much we actually know about our animal neighbours, and how drones, thermal cameras and artificial intelligence can give us a much better picture of where and how wildlife such as roe deer and hares live.

Christoph walked the audience through the whole chain of our drone-based wildlife monitoring: from aerial colour and thermal imagery, through the automatic detection and re-identification of animals, to determining their species, sex and age. With Airborne Light Field Sampling, we can even look through the forest canopy. These data feed into population surveys that show, for example, how the number of animals spotted changes over the course of a day. Applications range from population monitoring and carcass search to the prevention of wildlife-vehicle collisions.

The work presented comes from our projects [BAMBI]({{ '/projects/bambi/' | relative_url }}) and [THUMPER]({{ '/projects/thumper-2/' | relative_url }}). The takeaway of the evening: more surveillance of our natural habitats can help us understand and protect them better.
